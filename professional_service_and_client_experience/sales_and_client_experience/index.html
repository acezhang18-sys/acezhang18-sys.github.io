Sales & Client Experience

Overview

I close and keep work by treating the client as a short, high-stakes conversation: read the room, set a professional frame, solve the real objection, and leave the interaction rated. I have done this on doors, in a car, and on live platforms where the score is public and the next job depends on the last one.

Core Strengths

  • Build rapport fast without performing or oversharing
  • Separate mood from the task so the client still gets a clean outcome
  • Handle objections and entitled behavior without escalating
  • Keep presentation consistent: vehicle, tone, follow-through
  • Track the metrics that actually move money: rating, acceptance, repeat work, tip rate
  • Recover after a miss and take the next attempt instead of narrating the last one

Relevant Experience

  • Zion Capitol (field sales) — Field sales under time pressure. Learned that tone carries more than script. Used team priming and a simple self-check (“and that’s exactly what I want”) to keep energy usable across a long day. Practiced objection handling and the “next door” reset after a no.
  • Rideshare and private-client work — 1,300+ trips, 4.95 ride rating, ~90% delivery satisfaction, tip frequency well above the platform default. Prepared the vehicle as a hospitality product. Managed drunk, rushed, and difficult riders without turning the cabin into a fight. Converted at least one long private-client night from a public pickup when the rider needed continuity instead of a one-off drop.
  • Business Comfort and reserved rides — Acceptance rate and reserved-ride access treated as brand, not luck. Corrected delivery errors by tightening a three-check (instructions, order, address) instead of relying on personality to cover sloppy details.

Method

  • State before script. If the tone is off, the pitch will not save it.
  • Find the actual constraint: time, money, safety, ego. Speak to that, not to a canned opener.
  • Make the environment do part of the selling (clean car, on-time arrival, clear next step).
  • After a no or a 1-star, log the cause, fix the process, take the next job. Do not rehearse the loss.
  • Protect the rating the same way a shop protects a license. One sloppy drop costs more than one skipped cheap ride.

What This Means for Clients / Collaborators

I can represent a product or a service in person, keep the interaction professional when the other party is not, and leave a measurable trail (rating, repeat request, closed door) instead of a story about effort. Useful for field sales, client-facing ops, and any role where the next piece of work is earned in a five-minute window.
