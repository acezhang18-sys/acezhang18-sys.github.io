Professional Service & Client Experience

Overview

I deliver service the way a premium client actually experiences it: one person, full context, no handoff fog. High-touch means I read the room, own the outcome while they are in my care, and leave them better than the transaction required.

Core Method

  • Treat the person in front of me as the whole job, not a ticket
  • Notice state, time pressure, and unspoken constraints before I push a script
  • Stay present for the duration of the engagement instead of dumping them into a queue
  • Fix the real friction, then confirm the result in their language
  • End clean: they know what happened, what is next, and that someone was accountable

Relevant Experience

  • Zion Capitol (field sales) — Door-to-door work in a five-minute rapport window. Tone carried the sale more than the pitch. Learned to regulate my own state so the other person did not inherit anxiety, then close without making the interaction feel extracted.
  • Rideshare and private-client work — Uber/Lyft as high-touch logistics: timed pickups, airport and event pressure, riders who need a calm adult more than a lecture. Converted a World Cup night into a private-client run by staying with one person across venues instead of chasing the next ping.
  • Business Comfort and reserved rides — Higher-expectation trips where presentation, timing, and quiet professionalism are the product. Reliability is the service.
  • Tutoring and staff work — Diagnostic sessions and live community problems. The client is not buying a speech. They are buying a person who will stay in the problem until the next step is clear.
  • Team and community operations — Members and collaborators who only stay if the experience feels handled. Standards, follow-through, and de-escalation are service delivery, not extras.

Approach

    Before the interaction
    • Know the outcome they hired: arrival, a decision, a sale, a resolved mess
    • Put my own state in order. They will feel whatever I bring in
    During
    • Open with the human, then the task
    • Match pace. Do not dump process on someone already under load
    • Name what I am doing when it is not obvious, so they are not guessing
    • Protect the engagement: one owner, no “that’s not my department”
    After
    • Confirm the result in one line
    • Leave a next step or a clean close
    • Write down what broke so the next delivery is tighter

What This Means for Clients / Collaborators

I can take a high-expectation interaction — a client, a rider, a student, a partner — and run it as a single owned engagement. Useful anywhere the product is not only the task, but how it felt to be handled by a competent adult.
