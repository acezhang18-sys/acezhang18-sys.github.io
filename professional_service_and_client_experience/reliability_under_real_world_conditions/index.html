Reliability Under Real-World Conditions

Overview

Reliability is not a personality trait I perform when I am fresh. It is the ability to still deliver when the week is ugly: short sleep, thin margins, other people’s urgency, and no one coming to reset the board for me. I have been building that on purpose, in work that does not pause because I am tired.

Core Method

  • Decide the minimum that still counts as “done,” then protect it
  • Separate mood from output. The feeling can be bad. The drop-off still happens
  • Recover on a clock instead of waiting to “feel ready”
  • When capacity drops, cut scope, not the habit
  • Own the miss without turning it into a week-long story

Relevant Experience

  • Rideshare as the lab — Income only shows up if I start the app. Quests, boosts, and reserved Comfort trips punish inconsistenc.
  • Night-shift and sleep debt — Work that starts when my body wants to stop. I treat irritation after a short night as a recovery signal, not as proof the plan failed.
  • Zion Capitol field days — On my feet for hours after a two-hour morning cycle. The pitch still had to land after the body was already used.
  • Community and team ops — Servers and projects do not get to vanish because the founder is in a hole. Ownership handoffs, verification, and staff problems still need a decision the same day.
  • Constraint stacking — Knee limits, blood sugar management, roommate cashflow, and a market that goes soft midweek. The job is to keep a cadence inside those limits, not to wait for clean conditions.

Approach

Before the day
  • Name the one block that makes the day real
  • Eat and sleep like they are equipment
  • If yesterday was a crash, the goal is a start, not a record
During
  • Run the block. Do not renegotiate it every hour
  • If the body drops, cut hours. Keep the close
  • Do not use other people’s urgency as a reason to skip recovery you already scheduled
After
  • Log what actually happened, not what the mood claims
  • Reset day is a day. Next morning is the next one

What This Means for Clients / Collaborators

I can be used in conditions that are not a demo. Travel, events, field work, on-call logistics, and any role where the standard is “it still happens on a bad Tuesday.”
