Public Speaking & Online Streaming

Overview

I treat a live audience as a time-boxed delivery problem. The job is to keep people oriented: what they are looking at, why it matters, and what happens next. That holds for a festival booth, a shoutcast, a Discord stage, or a camera pitch. Energy is a tool. Clarity is the product.

Core Strengths

  • Running a live clock without freezing or filling the air with noise
  • Switching registers: hype for a match, plain language for a how-to, professional for a pitch
  • Giving the audience a map (stakes, players, next beat) before adding detail
  • Working a room that can talk back — chat, voice, or a live floor — without losing the thread
  • Recovering from a miss on mic: name it once, continue, do not audition for sympathy

Relevant Experience

  • NoVA Esports Cup — Organized and shoutcasted a live League of Legends tournament tied to the Herndon Festival: online qualifiers, in-person finals, on-site coordination with a PC cafe and festival staff. Commentary had to work for people standing in a public space, not only for players in the match.
  • Hirana Order — Built a casual-esports org and shoutcasted matches so average players got a broadcast-shaped experience. Repetition mattered more than a single “big” event: same standard, many sessions.
  • Community Voice and Stages — Briefings, tutoring blocks, and staff calls where the audience is small and the cost of rambling is high. Presence is structure: objective, demo, questions, close.
  • Camera-Facing Work — Short-form pitches and live-sale style delivery: product or seat in frame, objections answered in one breath, next step stated before the clock runs out.

Approach

  • Open with the frame: who this is for, what they will see, how long it takes
  • Talk to the back of the room. If a first-time viewer cannot follow, the regulars are already fine
  • One idea per beat. Then a pointer (score, map, price, next speaker)
  • Leave silence when the action is doing the work
  • End on an instruction, not a fade: where to go, what was decided, when the next block starts
