Languages & Linguistics

Overview

I treat language as an operating constraint, not a badge. English is the language I work in. Chinese family and cultural context is a second system I can read: what is said, what is left off the page, and what “yes” is doing in the room. Linguistics, for me, is the practical layer — register, high- vs low-context habits, and why the same sentence lands as clarity in one culture and as a threat in another.

Core Strengths

  • Writing and speaking professional English that survives a hiring panel, a client, and a public page
  • Switching register: plain instruction, formal email, live hype, staff brief
  • Reading high-context messages (face, implication, delay) without inventing a plot
  • Meeting a polite, indirect rider or client and still opening an American-direct, friendly line without flattening them
  • Explaining a technical idea in the listener’s vocabulary instead of mine
  • Noticing when a conflict is linguistic (direct vs indirect) before treating it as character

Languages

  • English — native. Working language for writing, teaching, sales, and operations.
  • Chinese(Mandarin)(Simplified) — fluent pronunciation, limited vocabulary. Enough for family register, tone, and social meaning; not a professional interpreter claim.
  • Spanish - functional. Enough set phrases to run a basic rideshare loop with Spanish-only riders (greeting, destination, wait, thank-you, problem).

Relevant Experience

  • Rideshare, Dallas-Fort Worth — Regularly carried riders from high-context cultures who default to polite, indirect speech. Job was to keep the trip clear and warm: I opened with American-style friendliness and honesty (time, route, "I want what you want, this is not a polite suggestion”) without treating their courtesy as evasiveness.
  • Working language — Docs, tutoring, sales, public pages, and live commentary in English.
  • Heritage context — Raised in a Chinese household. I can follow face, hierarchy, and what belongs in writing versus what belongs in the room.
  • Cross-register teaching — Material written so a first-pass reader can use it. In-jokes get rewritten.
  • Mixed rooms - Military, civilian, and international users. Lowest-ambiguity phrasing that still respects how that room talks.

Approach

  • Default to the explicit version when the cost of a miss is high (money, schedule, safety, hiring)
  • Use the indirect version when the other side will treat bluntness as contempt
  • Ice-breaker first, then the fact. Friendliness is not the same as padding
  • Stay inside true proficiency. Set phrases in Spanish; no fake fluency
  • Write for the person who will skim once on a phone

What This Means for Clients / Collaborators

I can run the job in native English, hear when high-context politeness is doing work in the sentence, and still put a clear, friendly American close on the interaction. Chinese and Spanish are operational, not credentials. The standard is that the other person can act, not that I collect languages.
