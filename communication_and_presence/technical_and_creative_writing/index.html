Technical & Creative Writing

Overview

Professional presence is how I take up space in a room without making the room about me. Soft skill is the work underneath that: reading the other person, matching the register, keeping the interaction usable when it is awkward, short, or high-stakes. I treat both as trainable systems, not as personality.

Core Strengths

  • Adjusting tone to the audience: client, staff, stranger in a car, hiring panel
  • Staying composed when the other side is blunt, late, or evaluating me
  • Making a first impression that is clean enough to continue the conversation
  • Listening for the actual request instead of answering the loudest sentence
  • Closing an interaction so the other person knows what happens next

Relevant Experience

  • Field sales (Zion Capitol): short windows to build rapport at the door, handle objections, and leave the conversation professional whether it closed or not.
  • Rideshare and delivery in Dallas-Fort Worth: hundreds of first meetings a week with no shared context. Presence is the product — vehicle, greeting, route, and how conflict is handled before it becomes a rating.
  • Live events and shoutcasting (NoVA Esports Cup, community broadcasts): energy on a clock, in front of people who did not come to hear a private monologue.
  • Community and staff rooms: setting a tone that staff can copy. Presence here is consistency, not volume.
  • Hiring and client conversations: translating irregular experience into plain language without overselling or collapsing into apology.

Approach

  • Arrive looking like I intended to be there (clock, clothes, setup).
  • Open at their pace. Match formality first; earn informality later.
  • One conversation, one job: understand, answer, or move the work. Do not unload the rest of the day onto them.
  • When I miss the register, correct in the next sentence. Do not narrate the miss.
  • Leave a clean last line: thanks, next step, or a clear no.

What This Means for Clients / Collaborators

I can be put in front of a customer, a partner, or a room and be trusted not to make the interaction smaller. The standard is not charisma. It is that the other person leaves knowing who I am, what I will do, and that the exchange was professional.
