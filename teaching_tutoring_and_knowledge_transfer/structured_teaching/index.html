Structured Teaching

Overview

I design instruction the way I design a system: objective first, sequence second, feedback third. Structured teaching means the learner can see the path, practice the hard part on purpose, and leave with something they can reuse without me in the room.

Core Method

  • Break a messy skill into a sequence with a clear pass/fail at each step
  • Teach from the test or the job backward, not from a pile of facts forward
  • Write the lesson so it survives the session: notes, checklists, worked examples
  • Adjust pace without abandoning the structure
  • Turn one good explanation into a guide other people can run

Relevant Experience

  • ASVAB and accession prep — Ran live tutoring sessions for prospective recruits. Built sessions around the score the person needed and the sections that were actually blocking them. One documented case: a candidate reached a 92 ASVAB and credited the session with landing a Navy ETN contract.
  • Pre-Selection curriculum — Turned field lessons into published guides (decision tools, note-taking under load, self-management, social presence) so the community was not dependent on a single voice channel. Instruction scaled from “I explained it once” to “the room can teach itself.”
  • Applied transfer — Took what I learned in high-tempo sales floors and wrote it back as teachable loops (reframe, next attempt, state before script) instead of leaving it as private folklore.

Method

  • Name the outcome in one line. If you cannot, you do not have a lesson yet.
  • Break the material into piecemeal, digestible pieces. I have done this with skills that look “innate,” including social skill: reverse-engineered it into small, actionable lessons instead of leaving it as personality.
  • Teach the minimum path that produces the result. Extra theory is homework, not the session.
  • Put a check in the middle: a problem, a drill, or a recap the learner has to produce.
  • Capture the structure in writing the same day. Memory is not a curriculum.
  • End with the next attempt, not with inspiration.

What This Means for Clients / Collaborators

I can take a skill people are waving their hands about, turn it into a short structured lesson, run it live, and leave a written version the next instructor can use. Useful for onboarding, test prep, and any team that keeps re-explaining the same thing from scratch.
