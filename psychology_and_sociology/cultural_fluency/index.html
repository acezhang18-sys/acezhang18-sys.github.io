Cultural Fluency

Overview

Cultural fluency is knowing which rule-set the other person is playing and answering in a way they can use. I do not flatten everyone into “be direct” or “be polite.” I diagnose the room: how disagreement is allowed, how status is shown, and whether a suggestion is actually a request. Then I choose the sentence that gets the job done without making them lose face or making me guess.

Core Strengths

  • Hearing when politeness is doing the work of a demand
  • Offering an explicit channel without treating their indirectness as dishonesty
  • Using a small set of cultural axes as a field checklist, not as a stereotype
  • Writing that checklist down so other people can run it
  • Staying in my own register when that is what the contract requires, and saying so plainly

Relevant Experience

  • Rideshare, Dallas-Fort Worth — Regular work with riders from high-context cultures. They often ask in the polite register (“maybe we could…,” “if it is not too much trouble…”). I treat that as a real request and open a low-context door: this is not a polite suggestion — tell me what you want so I can give you the best trip I can. Friendliness stays. Ambiguity does not.
  • Pre-Selection cultural analysis — Wrote field guides so aspirants and staff could adapt instead of projecting one country’s manners onto every room. Working axes included:
    • Individualist vs Collectivist
    • Low-Context vs High-Context
    • Egalitarian vs Meritocratic
    • Tight vs Loose norms
    • High vs Low power distance
      The point of the page was a fast read: how this group decides, how they say no, what “respect” looks like in that system.
  • Mixed military / civilian / international rooms — Same Discord, different default settings. Fluency is choosing whether the next line should be an order-shaped sentence, a consult, or a face-saving leave-behind.
  • Heritage context — Chinese family rules as a second operating system. Useful for recognizing face and hierarchy. Not used as a claim that every East Asian client is the same person.

Approach

  • Identify the rule-set from behavior (how they refuse, who they copy, what they apologize for)
  • Name my own default out loud when it would otherwise look like contempt: I am going to be specific so I can deliver
  • Give them a safe explicit prompt rather than punishing the indirect ask
  • Use the axes as questions, not as labels for a human
  • If I do not know the culture, I ask one clean question and follow their lead on formality

What This Means in Practice

I can take a polite, high-context client and still run an American service loop — honest times, honest constraints, honest “what do you actually want” — without stripping their dignity. I can also brief a team on why a room went sideways when the fight was cultural, not personal. The standard is a completed interaction both sides can stand.
