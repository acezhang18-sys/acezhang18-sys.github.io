Group Dynamics

Overview

A group is not a pile of individuals. It is a pattern: who sets the tone, who absorbs blame, what gets rewarded in public, and what happens when the founder is offline. I work the pattern on purpose — roles, norms, and the informal hierarchy — so the room stays pointed at the job instead of at each other.

Core Strengths

  • Seeing the real org chart (who people actually wait on) instead of the role list
  • Naming the loop a group is stuck in: scapegoat, status contest, silence-as-agreement
  • Designing a small structure that changes the loop (roles, vote path, who speaks last)
  • Keeping a mixed room from splitting into “insiders” and everyone else
  • Handing the room off so the dynamic is not my personality

Relevant Experience

  • Pre-Selection — Built a mixed civilian / service / aspirant room that accepted members from nations across the world. Dynamics problem was status and credibility, not activity. Verification, staff roles, and written norms were there so argument did not become the culture. Ownership moved on purpose so the group could not stay a founder cult.
  • Team Echoes / PDC — Creative group with elected player voice and seated leads. Dynamics work was balancing “the floor has a say” against “the work still ships.” Charter, recall, and approval bars exist because unchecked popularity and unchecked lead power both rot a team.
  • 38th and other unit-shaped communities — Selection on teamplay, not on volume. The dynamic I wanted was disciplined cooperation; toxicity was treated as a process failure, not as “just banter.”
  • Staff threads under heat — Two strong voices, a quiet majority, and a decision that still has to ship. My job is to keep the fight on the standard and off the person, then leave a record so the same fight does not restart tomorrow.

Approach

  • Ask what the group is optimizing for this week: speed, face, fairness, or control. They are not the same
  • Watch who gets interrupted and who gets quoted. That is the live hierarchy
  • Change one structural piece before giving a speech (who decides, who is in the room, what is written down)
  • Do not feed a scapegoat cycle. Move the heat onto a process or a standard
  • Exit tests the dynamic. If it collapses when I mute, it was never a group

What This Means in Practice

I can walk into a living group and tell you what it is actually doing — not what the pinned message says. I can then change a role, a rule, or a handoff so the next week is quieter and more productive. The standard is a room that still works when the loudest person, including me, is gone.
