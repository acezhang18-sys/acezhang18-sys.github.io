Long-Term Vision & Adaptation

Overview

I can hold a multi-year direction without freezing the plan. The vision is a destination and a standard. The path is allowed to change when the market, the body, or the team data says the old route is closed.

Core Strengths

  • Keep a stable “what we are building” while swapping methods
  • Drop a path that is no longer viable without discarding the people or the skill
  • Convert a setback into a constraint list, then a next experiment
  • Build systems that still work after the founder steps back
  • Learn a new domain fast enough that the long plan does not stall

What This Looks Like in Practice

  • Career Span — More than a decade across software engineering, team lead work, field sales, and real-time operations. Same through-line: ship, teach, and leave the next person a system. The job titles changed; the standard did not.
  • Team Echoes — Multi-year venture through funding droughts. Held the product thesis, cut cost (~80% via sweat equity and a Player Design Committee), and documented systems so the work was not hostage to one developer.
  • Leadership Handoff — Stood up Pre-Selection and later transferred ownership on purpose so the group could run without the founder as the bottleneck. Same pattern in organized community command structure: build, raise the next lead, step back.
  • Domain shifts under constraint — Enterprise Java and rules engines (Accenture / VA GI Bill, IBM ODM) to door-to-door sales (Zion Capitol) to high-volume rideshare operations. Each move was a response to a closed door, not a personality reset.
  • Written planning tools —
    • VECTOR (Value, Effort, Collateral, Timeliness, Odds, Readability) for choosing among paths.
    • 20% rule (plan ~120% of the known requirement) so a long goal survives contact with traffic, fatigue, and missing inputs.

Method

  • Separate aim from route. Aim stays stable longer than route.
  • Review on a cadence, not on a mood. If the metric is dead for a quarter, change the method, not the story.
  • Prefer options that leave residual skill even if the bet fails (teaching, documentation, operations reps).
  • Hand off early enough that the vision is not a personality cult.
  • “It’s the next one”: close the miss, take the data, run the next iteration. No monument to the failed plan.

What This Means in Practice

Can hold a three-to-five-year picture and still take this month’s honest job. Useful when a team or a career has to change shape without losing the point of the work.
